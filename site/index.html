01 / The problem

A familiar name.
An unfamiliar destination.

A link can borrow the appearance of a name you know. Some letters in different writing systems look remarkably alike. Swapping one can create an entirely different domain.

These are called homograph attacks. The characters are real. The deception is in how they’re used.

FAMILIAR SPELLING

paypal.com

→
TWO LETTERS REPLACED

раypal.com

The second example starts with Cyrillic р and а. These examples are displayed as text, so there’s nothing to accidentally visit.

※

Different languages aren’t the problem. Unicode makes a multilingual internet possible. A non-Latin letter isn’t evidence of fraud; context and the exact address matter.

03 / The project

Small details.
Shared responsibility.

GlyphGuardian began with a simple idea: make character-level deception easier to recognize. Created by Alec / WhyTrashEarth, it brings security, design, and accessibility into the same conversation.

This website and character lab are an early step. Research is ongoing, and browser protection remains a development goal.
